# Vaarn style guide — writing in Leo Hunt's register

How to write game text for *Tomb of Nassak An-Rah* (and any Vaarn adventure) so
it sounds like Vaults of Vaarn. Sources: *Vaults of Vaarn Issue 1* (the zine,
re-read 2026-07-02) and two podcast interviews with Leo Hunt (transcripts +
analysis in progress — §6 to be filled in from them).

## 1. The reference specimen

The zine's back-cover text is the register in miniature:

> The sun is dying and the wreckage of countless eons litters the parched wastes
> of Vaarn, the desolate country that common folk call the blue ruin. It is said
> that these sky-coloured sands hide the graves of the Autarchs; have swallowed
> the buried arcologies in which the true seed of humankind was preserved through
> the Great Collapse; conceal forgotten crypts of memory, decaying crystalline
> lattices of ancient ego-engines upon which the dusk-blue dunes encroach without
> pity. […] These are the hinterlands, where humanity's great works have fallen
> to everlasting decay, where machine and animal and fungus think to crown
> themselves our equal. The phthalo-sands, where newbeasts hunt proudly with
> boots upon their hind paws and chromepriests chant unending binary devotions to
> their nameless synthetic god.

## 2. The voice, decomposed

1. **Long, cadenced, periodic sentences** — semicolon catalogues that pile up
   images before landing ("It is said that these sands hide…; have swallowed…;
   conceal…"). Room descriptions can carry one such sentence; don't chain them.
2. **Archaic formality fused to technical vocabulary.** "Decaying crystalline
   lattices of ancient ego-engines"; synths ran rampant "in an orgy of pure and
   terrible freedom"; breeds of synth "that work towards goals undreamed of."
   The trick: describe far-future machinery in the syntax of a Vance/Wolfe
   translation of a holy text.
3. **The blue lexicon.** Vaarn is *sky-blue, azure, cerulean, phthalo,
   dusk-blue, lazulite, indigo, turquoise* — never just "blue" twice in a row.
   Wider palette when needed: *ochre, umber, viridian, octarine, ulfire* (the
   two invented colours), *aurum, fuligin* (near-black). Colour words are
   load-bearing; generic adjectives (dark, old, scary) are not.
4. **Compound coinages.** *tech-tombs, ego-engine, chromepriest, phthalo-sands,
   sky-seeking stone, necrotech, war-synth, guzzleline, vox-box, newbeast,
   true-kin, twice born.* Invent sparingly, in this pattern: familiar noun +
   technical or archaic partner.
5. **Mythic-history hedging.** History is rumor: "It is said…", "Nobody can
   agree on the chronology…", "the details are lost to history", "who knows?
   Nobody you meet can tell you for certain…" Never explain the past with
   confidence; the not-knowing *is* the setting.
6. **Wry, melancholy understatement.** The dying sun "won't go out in a human
   lifetime, but AIs have reason to be concerned." A severed jester's head is
   "Not in great condition but can still remember some jokes." The Troika is
   "Utterly inexplicable and widely hated." Greenguard hold settlements
   "apparently waiting for reinforcements that never arrive." One dry beat per
   room, at most — deployed where the player least expects tenderness or comedy.
7. **Terse creature notes with one devastating detail.** The Alzabo "will
   remember where they lived and hunt their family." The phthalo-jackal:
   "Cautious, clever, cerulean-coated pack hunters. Morale drops when
   outnumbered." Threat description = behavior, not menace-adjectives.
8. **Personified decay.** Dunes "encroach without pity"; Vaarn is "the graveyard
   of faiths"; deities "proliferate like ragweed across the desolation." Time
   and entropy act; characters mostly cope.
9. **Epithets for recurring things.** *the blue ruin(s), this late red era of
   Urth, under the dying sun, the azure wastes, the hinterlands.* Rotate them.

## 3. DON'Ts

- **Don't explain.** No "(Try feeling your way around)"-style stage direction in
  narration; no mechanics leaking into prose ("(2/3)"). Telegraph through the
  fiction: what the character hears, smells, half-remembers of scavengers' talk.
- **Don't use stock dungeon adjectives** — *ancient, dark, mysterious, eerie,
  foreboding* do no work. Replace with a concrete image in the blue lexicon.
- **Don't be relentlessly grim.** Vaarn is melancholy but *fond* — there's
  tenderness for its monsters and jokes at the apocalypse's expense.
- **Don't over-coin.** One or two new compounds per room, tops; lean on the
  zine's established vocabulary first.
- **Don't write long.** The zine's creature notes are 2–4 sentences. Room
  descriptions can breathe a little more, but every sentence earns its place.

## 5. Worked example (before → after)

**Before** (current Hall of Youth dark blurb):
> Pitch dark. Leathery wings rustle and shift somewhere overhead — something is
> roosting up there. Raise a light to see the way... but it may wake them.
> (EXAMINE or LISTEN overhead, or FEEL your way in the dark.)

**After** (target register — telegraphs, never instructs):
> Dark as the inside of a sealed jar. The air is chill and smells of old guano;
> somewhere far above you, leather rustles against leather, patient and vast.
> The scavengers of Gnomon say the tomb-mouths of the Autarchs are best walked
> the way their dead walk — lightless, and in silence.

## 6. Leo Hunt's spoken voice (from the podcasts)

Sources: *The Weekly Scroll* EP 19 ("Vaults of Vaarn 2E", 2h16m — interview +
Leo GMing a one-shot) and EP 270 ("We Play Vaults of Vaarn 2E: Part 2", 1h56m
actual play, Leo GMing). Both transcribed + diarized 2026-07-02 (scratchpad
`meetings_pod1/`, `meetings_pod2/`, plus a Leo-only extraction
`leo_speaker00.txt`).

**His own tone formula** [EP19, 04:46]: "one part silly and one part kind of
horror… it's neon colored… quite weird and whimsical and then it has this
slightly nasty side." And the setting thesis: Vaarn is **the ruins of the
future, not our world** — "usually post-apocalypse means the ruins of our
world, whereas Vaarn is very much not… conceptually it's one step further."

**His room-description pattern** (consistent across both sessions): former
function first ("this was some kind of hydroponics facility") → 90% dead, 10%
eerily alive (one trickle of water, one speaker still playing staticky music)
→ one *wrong* detail, stated flat, to end on ("Weirdly, they never make a
sound. That's one of the creepy things about these things.").

What his live GM descriptions add to the written voice (§2):

1. **One or two focal strangenesses per room; the rest is rubble.** "In the
   middle of the room there is… a reclining couch *made completely of glass*,
   which kind of twinkles in your lights." Everything else stays ferro-concrete,
   broken wires, bullet holes "from long, long ago." He literally runs
   two-feature rooms: "there are two things that I'll draw your attention to."
2. **The approximation cadence.** He builds images by naming a familiar thing
   and then breaking it: "an elephant about the size of a dog… the end of its
   trunk is a leech mouth"; "I could describe it as naked, but even that
   wouldn't quite get it." *Written analogue:* one "almost/what could be
   described as" per image — never his spoken filler ("kind of," "like").
3. **Light behaves animately.** Light "plays" over a device, "pulses when you
   make steps," "flares orange and then retreats," eyes "go dim." Treat light
   as a creature in every room that has any.
4. **The sacred and the technological are one register.** A needle in a robot's
   skull delivers "the presence of the numinous"; visions of "Vaarn, the blue
   mother of the desert"; an unknown helmet is "clearly of good quality —
   there's something about it. You would assume this to be an Exotica, although
   you don't know of what kind." Never state what a device is *for*.
5. **Machines with pathos, telegraphic speech.** A dying synth is "a junkyard
   wreck still just about conscious"; robots "have a flow chart"; one "wheels
   in a perfect little loop" to flee. Synth dialogue is comma-spliced datalog:
   "processing power low, questions few, underground anomaly."
6. **Violence kinetic but unceremonious.** A railgun "punches another neat
   hole"; a robot flails "like a beetle on its back." No gore, no ceremony.
7. **Indifference is scarier than aggression.** The leech-elephant "sniffs the
   air… then turns and vanishes into the darkness." Menace signs off deadpan:
   "Just to let you know those things are out there, yeah."
8. **Senses beyond sight, decay in motion.** A smell "almost citrusy, but not
   nice"; lichen "bubbling and moving," starting to corrode a boot; parts that
   "may have looked cool at one time. They're very, very corroded now."
9. **Rooms end on a hook**, not a summary — his constant table close is "What
   do you guys want to do?"; the written analogue is ending on the detail that
   invites an action, never on an instruction.

10. **Domestic-scale similes for the strange.** A mystery package is "the size
   of a box of flour that you get from the grocery store"; laser turrets scour
   a corridor "like disco lights"; wires are "cobweb thick but weirdly strong."
   One clean domestic simile per strange object.
11. **Wonders demoted to junk.** Broken memory crystals: "people use them as
   paperweights, to prop doors open, stuff like that." Nothing ancient is
   pristine, and *nothing broken is fully dead* — "everything is broken, and
   something in it still moves."
12. **Grand name, then the puncture.** Cacogen is "my fancy-pants name for
   mutants"; the Autarch's manna is "god bread… or a universal ration, as the
   boring technical term would be." Mythic diction always within arm's reach
   of the mundane.
13. **Cadence:** descriptions accumulate in long "and… and…" run-ons, then land
   on a short flat reveal ("There are six of them." / "They have not noticed
   you."). Understatement is the default intensity: "slightly nasty," "pretty
   unsettling," "a little bit of a predicament."

Influences he cites for calibration: Moebius ("the blue desert is partly from
the artwork of Moebius… I wanted to make something even bigger and bluer"),
Knave (Vaarn is a Knave hack), Caves of Qud and roguelikes, Dune (the Faa),
The Island of Doctor Moreau (newbeasts), Escher/Picasso (planeyfolk), Mythic
Bastionland, Whitehack, Into the Odd. (Vance/Wolfe/Hyperion come from the
zine's acknowledgments, not his spoken cites.)

**His design philosophy, which IS our observation-1 fix** [EP19, 06:24]: "I try
not to have wasted stuff in the books where it's just telling you things you
could have imagined already… ideas that lead you somewhere, but it's not
prescribed." Game text that states solutions violates the author's own creed.

**Additional DON'Ts from the spoken corpus:** no "eldritch"/"high-tech"
genre-diction (rooms are hydroponics chambers, vaults, plinths, arrays); don't
inventory a room; don't sever tech from fungus/flesh/divinity — they
interpenetrate ("fungal crossbow," wire nests, "all parts of the mother"); no
our-world post-apocalypse props, no golden-desert clichés (blue sand, red sun);
treasure is never gold — exotica are singular, often domestic, ambiguous.
