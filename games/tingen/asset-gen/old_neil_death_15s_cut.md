# Old Neil Death — 5-Clip Generation Cut (60s)

Each clip is one continuous AI video generation, capped at 15 seconds. Five clips total. The hard cuts live at the clip seams, parked on the four biggest shocks: the door-bleed return, the reveal swap, the first gunshot, and Dunn's button. Keeping the first gunshot as a clip seam is what keeps the kill off-screen — a 4-clip version forces the plea, the gunshots, and the aftermath into one take, which renders the kill on-screen as muzzle flashes.

Style token carried in every prompt: dawn-grey painterly, desaturated, single arterial-red accent.

The five locked design pillars, preserved throughout:
1. The piano is the dial — Celeste's tune is the emotional gauge, and its tuning (not just volume) tells the story.
2. Red is the only villain in the palette — dawn-grey desaturation until the water reddens, then red climbs; at the end the two crystalline eyes are the single saturated object left.
3. Never show the kill — peak on Klein's trembling gun-hand and recoiling face; both gunshots off-screen.
4. The big reveal is the in-place normal-to-lost_control room swap, never a new place; no ceiling tilt-up.
5. Dunn's line is the button — flat, tired, eyes off the body; the melody severs on his last word.

## Piano dial across the clips

The piano's tuning, not just its volume, is the through-line and survives every seam by audio, making five generations read as one score.

Clip 1: diegetic in-tune through the wood, blooms to full score plus cello (warmest, dial open).
Clip 2: full score, one sour/detuned note exactly as the water reddens, fully detuned by the door-open red face-wash.
Clip 3: the lone ~1s low-end hit at the eruption, then starved/near-silent, fully detuned through the bloom, un-bends ring by ring to Celeste's true melody as the box "3-0611" lands and frost spreads.
Clip 4: true melody resolves into one sustained, unresolved chord, held through the plea and tremble.
Clip 5: that chord snaps with a string-break on the first gunshot (carried across the 4-to-5 seam), then lone music-box-fragile piano through the aftermath, severed mid-phrase on Dunn's last word, hard to black.

The two hardest tuning events are deliberately parked on the two strongest seams: the un-bend on the box-frost (within Clip 3, anchoring the false calm) and the string-break on the first gunshot (on the 4-to-5 seam, where the kill happens off-screen).

## What changed from the 12-shot cut

- Shots 1 and 2 (door macro plus back-view wide plus dolly to Dunn) fused into one continuous retreating crane (Clip 1). The three Nighthawks are now revealed by the pull-back, not cut to.
- Shots 3 to 5 (bleeding door, Klein at seam, door opens onto three faces) — three separate hard cuts — folded into one continuous threshold creep (Clip 2). The three faces are reframed by a pivot onto present actors, never a cut-to.
- Shots 6 to 8 (reveal swap, reaction smear, Quiet Hair frost) become one locked top-down in-place morph (Clip 3). The withheld reveal is delivered as the 2-to-3 seam, opening on near-black then lighting into the normal plate. No ceiling tilt, no new place.
- Shot 9 (plea) plus the gun-hand stay as one continuous tilt-down (Clip 4). Neil's plea and Dunn's button are both handled as framed inserts — underlit eyes and mouth / non-speaking profile with the line as voiceover — because a fully lip-synced speaking face is what image-to-video does worst.
- Shots 10 to 12 (the kill, drained aftermath, Dunn's button) re-split: the first gunshot is the 4-to-5 seam (kill never shown), and recoil-to-top-down-to-Dunn becomes Clip 5. The second gunshot is authored inside Clip 5 so the frame-late flinch never desyncs across a stitch. The maw stays an off-frame red smear; both bullets stay off-frame.

## If you need to drop to 4 clips

Don't, unless a tool hard-limits you to 4 generations. Collapsing to 4 forces the plea-face, Klein's face, the two gunshots, the top-down aftermath, and Dunn's profile into one take — a disguised internal hard cut across two different spaces that renders the kill on-screen as muzzle flashes, violating the never-show-the-kill pillar. Keeping 5 is what makes the gunshot a clean seam. If forced to 4, merge Clips 4 and 5 only by accepting the kill as an off-screen flash inside one take, a real compromise. Otherwise the 5-clip cut is the deliverable.

## Production note

Image-to-video models are weakest at exactly the two things this cut leans on: a legible human face (Neil's plea, Klein's recoil, Dunn) and a clean in-place texture morph (Clip 3). Clip 3 is backed by real art (neil_home_normal.png and neil_home_lost_control.png), so it is the safest. The face beats are the risk. An in-engine fallback for Clip 3 exists: RoomState already swaps normal-to-lost_control, and an IntroCard-style two-sprite alpha-tween gives the reality-wavers dissolve.
